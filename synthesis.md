# Problem Statement: Improving Diabetes Care Conversations

## Target Audience

People with diabetes who want to have better, more productive conversations with their endocrinologist and healthcare team about their diabetes management and care plan.

## Current Challenges

Many people with diabetes feel as though they are being interrogated during appointments when healthcare providers ask questions such as:

- Why their glucose numbers have been inconsistent.
- What may have caused specific highs or lows.
- Details about what they were doing on a particular day or event several weeks or months ago.

These conversations can become frustrating because patients are often expected to recall highly specific details from a random day long after it has passed.

## Why This Happens

Managing everyday life is already challenging for most people. For someone living with diabetes, those normal daily responsibilities are combined with the constant attention required to manage:

- Blood glucose levels
- Medication schedules
- Insulin dosing
- Meals and nutrition
- Physical activity
- Stress and illness management

Over time, the large amount of information and decision-making required can become overwhelming. As days, weeks, and months pass, individual events begin to blend together, making it difficult to remember the details of a specific day when discussing past glucose patterns with a healthcare provider.

This ongoing mental burden can contribute to:

- Diabetes burnout
- Reduced engagement in self-management tasks
- Difficulty remembering historical context behind glucose readings
- Frustration during medical appointments
- Less productive conversations with healthcare providers

## Opportunity

There is an opportunity to create tools, systems, or workflows that help people with diabetes capture meaningful context around their daily diabetes management. By making it easier to remember what was happening when glucose patterns occurred, patients can have more informed and collaborative discussions with their healthcare team, leading to better-supported care decisions.
